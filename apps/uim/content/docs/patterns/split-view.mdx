`SplitView` lays out up to three panes: `SplitViewSidebar`, `SplitViewList` and `SplitViewDetail`. When the window is at least `breakpoint` wide (768 by default), they sit side by side in a bordered row, sized 20, 32 and 48 parts.

Below the breakpoint only the current column is shown, full screen and scrollable, with a large title. Pressing a `SplitViewItem` moves to the next column, and a back button titled with the previous pane returns to it. On Android the hardware back button also goes back. Use it for mail, notes or settings screens that drill down.

## Usage

<Code lang="tsx" code={"import { SplitView, SplitViewSidebar, SplitViewList, SplitViewDetail, SplitViewItem } from \"@/components/patterns/split-view\""} />

<Code lang="tsx" code={"<MailExample />"} />

Give each pane a `title`; the back button only appears when the previous pane exists and has a title. Put `SplitViewItem`s in the sidebar and list panes, and use their `onPress` to set the selection in your own state.

The current column is uncontrolled with `defaultColumn` (`list` by default) or controlled with `column` and `onColumnChange`. `useSplitView` returns `isCompact`, `column` and `setColumn` for custom navigation.

## Accessibility

- Pane titles have the `heading` role, and panes are labelled by string titles.
- On phones, screen reader focus moves to the new pane's heading after navigating.
- `SplitViewItem` exposes its `selected` state.
- Back and row chevrons mirror in right-to-left layouts.

## Notes

- On phones the new pane fades in, respecting the system reduce motion setting.
- Active rows use `bg-surface-secondary`; panes are divided by `border-separator`.
