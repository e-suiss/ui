`QRScanner` is a `View` that draws an overlay on top of whatever you pass as `children`, usually a camera preview. It does not open the camera itself. The overlay dims everything outside a square frame (drawn with `react-native-svg`), marks the frame with four corner brackets and animates a scan line with Reanimated while the state is `idle`.

The frame size defaults to 68% of the shorter side, capped at 280 points, and is centered in the component. A `title` sits above the frame; a `description` and the flashlight button sit below it.

## Usage

<Code lang="tsx" code={"import { QRScanner } from \"@/components/ui/qr-scanner\""} />

<Code lang="tsx" code={"<Scanner description=\"Point your camera at a code to pay or connect.\" />"} />

Render your camera view as `children` and size the scanner with `className`. When there are no children, the background falls back to `bg-media`.

Set `state` to `success` or `error` after you read a code. The brackets change color, and `error` shakes the frame. Pass `onTorchToggle` together with a controlled `torch` value to show the flashlight button; leave it out to hide the button. `title` and `description` accept strings or your own elements.

The `QRScannerState` type is also exported.

## Accessibility

- The dimmed surround and brackets are hidden from VoiceOver and TalkBack.
- The overlay is a polite live region, so changes to the title and description are announced on Android.
- The flashlight button has the `button` role, uses `torchLabel` as its label and reports `torch` as its selected state.
- State changes to `success` and `error` play a matching haptic notification.
- The shake and scan-line animations respect the system reduce motion setting.

## Notes

- Uses the `bg-media`, `text-scrim`, `text-on-media`, `border-success`, `border-danger` and `bg-accent` tokens.
- The scan line restarts whenever the state returns to `idle`.
