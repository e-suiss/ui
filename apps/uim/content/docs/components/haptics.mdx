Components such as Amount Input, Chart and Context Menu play haptic feedback through `useHaptics()`. By default nothing happens: `HapticsProvider` lets you plug in an adapter that calls the haptics library of your choice, so the components stay free of a native dependency.

The adapter has three methods: `impact(style)` with `light`, `medium`, `heavy`, `soft` or `rigid`; `selection()`; and `notification(type)` with `success`, `warning` or `error`.

## Usage

<Code lang="tsx" code={"import { HapticsProvider } from \"@/components/ui/haptics\""} />

<Code lang="tsx" code={"<ControlsDemo />"} />

Wrap your app in `HapticsProvider` and pass an `adapter` object that implements `impact`, `selection` and `notification`. Set `enabled={false}` to turn all feedback off, for example from a user setting.

Call `useHaptics()` in your own components to get the same methods. Without a provider, or when disabled, they do nothing.

## Notes

- The types `HapticsAdapter`, `HapticsImpactStyle` and `HapticsNotificationType` are exported for typing your adapter.
- Haptics have no visual output; pair important feedback with a visible change.
