`Stepper` recreates the iOS stepper on top of a number field primitive. It pairs an editable `StepperInput` with a segmented `StepperGroup` of `StepperDecrement` and `StepperIncrement` buttons. Each press changes the value by `step`; holding a button repeats the change after a short delay and speeds up over time. Each button disables itself when the value reaches `min` or `max`.

Use it for small, bounded quantities such as copies, servings, or scale, where people usually adjust a value by a few steps.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Stepper, StepperInput, StepperGroup, StepperDecrement, StepperIncrement, StepperSeparator } from "@/components/interactions/stepper"
```

```tsx
<Stepper defaultValue={1} min={0} max={10}>
  <StepperInput aria-label="Quantity" />
  <StepperGroup>
    <StepperDecrement />
    <StepperSeparator />
    <StepperIncrement />
  </StepperGroup>
</Stepper>
```

- Compose `StepperInput` and a `StepperGroup` containing `StepperDecrement`, `StepperSeparator`, and `StepperIncrement` inside `Stepper`.
- The value is uncontrolled by default; pass `value` and `onValueChange` to control it.
- Other number field props, such as `min`, `max`, `disabled`, and `format`, pass through to the root.
- The buttons render minus and plus icons by default; pass children to replace them.

## Accessibility

- Label `StepperInput` with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.
- The arrow keys step the value from the input, as provided by the number field primitive.
- The buttons are labelled `Decrease` and `Increase`, and are disabled at the limits or when the stepper is disabled.
- `StepperGroup` has `role="group"`, and `StepperSeparator` is `aria-hidden`.

## Notes

- Parts use `data-slot` values `stepper`, `stepper-input`, `stepper-group`, `stepper-decrement`, `stepper-increment`, and `stepper-separator`.
- Values are rounded to the precision of `step`, so decimal steps do not accumulate floating-point errors.
- The buttons suppress the long-press context menu so holding them on touch devices keeps stepping.

`Stepper` is a number field laid out as a right-aligned value followed by a field holding minus and plus buttons, inset from its edges. Each step snaps to `step` and stays within `min` and `max`, and a button that can go no further is shown as disabled.

Pressing and holding a button steps once, then repeats every 60 ms after 400 ms. The value can also be typed. Use it for quantities, copies or percentages in settings rows.

```tsx
import { Stepper, StepperInput, StepperGroup, StepperDecrement, StepperIncrement, StepperSeparator } from "@/components/interactions/stepper"
```

```tsx
<Stepper defaultValue={1} min={0} max={10}>
  <StepperInput accessibilityLabel="Quantity" />
  <StepperGroup>
    <StepperDecrement />
    <StepperSeparator />
    <StepperIncrement />
  </StepperGroup>
</Stepper>
```

Compose `StepperInput` and a `StepperGroup` containing `StepperDecrement`, `StepperSeparator` and `StepperIncrement` inside `Stepper`.

Use it uncontrolled with `defaultValue` (`0` by default) or controlled with `value` and `onValueChange`. Clearing the field never reports `null`; the change is cancelled instead. `min`, `max`, `step`, `format` (`Intl.NumberFormatOptions`), `locale` and `disabled` are passed to the number field.

## Accessibility

- The minus and plus buttons have the `button` role.
- Label the value with `accessibilityLabel` or `accessibilityLabelledBy` on `StepperInput`.
- The separator is hidden from screen readers.

## Notes

- A selection haptic plays on each step, if the app provides an adapter through `HapticsProvider`.
- The group uses `bg-secondary`. Buttons use `bg-card` with a small shadow (`bg-border` in dark mode) and `opacity-80` when pressed; a disabled button or one at its limit uses `opacity-50`.
- Disabled text uses `opacity-50`.
