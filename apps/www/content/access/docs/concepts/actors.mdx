---
title: Parties, Instances and agents
description: Who acts in Access. Parties hold authority, Instances act, capacity says on whose behalf, and agents use the same model as people.
---

Access separates **who holds authority** from **what is acting right now**. A person or company holds authority; a specific session, device or agent run uses it.

## Party

A **Party** is a person or organization with rights and responsibilities: a human, a company, an agent, a service.

- Access refers to a Party through a **PartyRef**. Access does not keep a second, global registry of Parties.
- Organizations and jointly owned groups do not act directly. Their authority is exercised by an Instance acting **for** the organization.
- Outside its own authority domain, a Party is shown under a **domain-pairwise pseudonym**: stable within one domain, unrelated across domains. Linking a Party across domains requires an explicit, recorded binding.

Example: a person; a company; the company's purchasing agent.

## Instance

An **Instance** is the concrete acting occurrence of a Party: a session on a given device, or a running agent process.

- It is the actor of every authority exercise and the target of a Mandate.
- Its identity is the continuity of its key binding. Rotating keys keeps the same Instance; breaking continuity creates a new one.
- Recovery always produces a single **successor Instance**. The successor does not inherit the old Instance's Mandates; rebinding them is an explicit step.

Three things are kept apart:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| InstanceID | The logical identity of the Instance in Access |
| KeyBinding | The current proof-of-possession material (for example a DPoP or mutual-TLS key) |
| Attestation | A claim about the runtime environment of an Instance or key |

### Instance lifecycle

| State | Reached by |
|---|---|
| active | `instance.create` (genesis ceremony) |
| restricted | A restriction overlay; can return to active |
| active (rekeyed) | `instance.rekey`, authenticated with the current key binding |
| terminated | `instance.terminate` |
| recovered | `instance.recover`, which creates one successor Instance |
| expired | Its validity ends |

## Actor context

Every request carries an actor context:

- **`Authenticated(InstanceID)`**: the request carries an Actor Intent Statement signed with the Instance's current key binding.
- **`Anonymous(context)`**: no statement is present. The context records only actor-independent facts such as channel or network. An anonymous actor holds nothing, cannot approve and cannot change authority. It can only use public Grants.

The service that forwards a request (the policy enforcement point, or PEP) is never the actor. Its own transport identity, such as a client certificate or a DPoP key, never becomes an actor context.

## Capacity: on whose behalf

Every exercise declares a **capacity**:

| Capacity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `own` | The actor's Party acts on its own behalf |
| `for(P)` | The actor's Party acts on behalf of Party P |

The declared capacity must be one the Grant being used allows. On-behalf-of is never inferred from the delegation chain or from a token; it comes only from this explicit declaration. A Grant can only allow `own` and `for` the principal who issued it, so an organization cannot consent on a user's behalf through its own Grant.

"Principal" is a role, not a stored entity: it is the Party named by the capacity.

## Agents

An AI agent is not a separate security universe. People, services, workloads and agents use the same authority model.

- In the authority plane an agent is an **Instance** under a Party. That Party can be the agent's operator, the user's own agent Party, or an organization.
- The agent's authority comes only from Grants. Its Mandate can only narrow what it may use; it never adds authority.
- An action `for(P)` without a Grant whose agency allows `for(P)` is denied.
- A model provider holds no authority by default.
- Agent kind, operator and sponsor are recorded as claims, not as authority. Relations such as `operated-by` and `sponsored-by` are claims between Parties.

Three levels are kept separate:

| Level | Access object |
|---|---|
| The agent "product" | An operator Party plus a Grant template |
| A running copy, run or task | An Instance: the unit of attribution and revocation |
| A single action | An authority exercise, identified by its nonce |

Templates for agents are designed to require a budget, limit further delegation and keep agent projections short-lived. These are policy defaults, not separate semantics.

The thesis behind this: take authorization out of the model and put it in a deterministic layer. Prompt injection cannot be solved in the authorization layer. What that layer can do is limit what a compromised agent can do to the intersection of its Grant, its Mandate and its budget.

## Support access, without impersonation

There is no impersonation in Access. A support agent always acts with their own Instance, on behalf of their own Party. On a user's resources they can act only through:

- a Grant `for(user)` that the user issued themselves, which can be revoked and cascades, or
- a break-glass Grant issued in advance for that purpose.

Every support action is attributed to the support agent's own Instance.
